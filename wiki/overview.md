# Overview

**insight-notes** is a personal LLM Wiki, or “second brain.” Connected markdown pages in `wiki/` build knowledge over time. Original sources stay unchanged in `raw/`.

## Current shape

The wiki has twelve content layers:

1. **Meta** — How the wiki works and grows ([[sources/001-llm-wiki-pattern|LLM Wiki pattern]]).
2. **Indonesia’s economy and available money** — [[sources/002-endgame-245-purbaya-sadewa|Endgame #245]] features [[entities/purbaya-yudi-sadewa|Purbaya]]. It covers money growth, public expectations, a task force for removing investment barriers, and a path to 8% growth.
3. **Indonesia’s government and social foundations** — [[sources/003-endgame-248-tom-lembong|Endgame #248]] features [[entities/tom-lembong|Tom Lembong]]. It covers teachers, the decentralization paradox, wider ownership, attitudes toward risk, and political debate around the center.
4. **Science and intelligence** — [[sources/004-endgame-193-michael-levitt|Endgame #193]] features [[entities/michael-levitt|Michael Levitt]]. It covers biological intelligence, diversity as a survival strategy, barriers between academic fields, and discovery through luck and failure. COVID receives limited attention.
5. **Expert-led government and fair business advancement** — [[sources/005-endgame-272-martyn-terpilowski|Endgame #272]] features [[entities/martyn-terpilowski|Martyn Terpilowski]]. It compares advancement by merit with advancement through connections. It also covers attracting foreign investment and talent, plus long-term deep technology. [[entities/bhumi-varta-technology|Bhumi Varta]] is presented as a counterexample.
6. **Indonesia’s budget, middle class, and foreign relations** — [[sources/006-endgame-216-chatib-basri|Endgame #216]] features [[entities/chatib-basri|Chatib Basri]]. It covers pressure on the middle class, tax compliance, and foreign direct investment when policy options are limited. It also discusses Government 4.0 and ASEAN balancing among several global powers.
7. **AI infrastructure and national control** — [[sources/007-bravo-500-ai-panel|Bravo 500 panel]] features [[entities/budi-gunadi-sadikin|Budi]], [[entities/franky-widjaja|Franky]], and [[entities/sai-prasad|Sai]]. It covers the electricity needed to build AI, two forms of data control, and adapting proven applications locally.
8. **Consciousness, elites, and media** — [[sources/008-endgame-259-jiang-xueqin|Endgame #259]] features [[entities/jiang-xueqin|Jiang Xueqin]]. It treats attention as wealth and discusses Techno-Marxism, too many competing elites, and fractured journalism. Speculative claims about geopolitics receive limited coverage.
9. **China’s political economy** — [[sources/009-endgame-239-keyu-jin|Endgame #239]] features [[entities/keyu-jin|Keyu Jin]]. It explains the [[concepts/mayor-economy|mayor economy]], capable entrepreneurial government, and innovation in processes and crises. US–China rivalry and Southeast Asia’s chance to direct technology investment are secondary topics.
10. **AI industry strategy in the United States and China** — [[sources/010-indrawan-perang-ai-china-vs-amerika|Indrawan #010]] explains the DeepSeek shock. It separates [[concepts/value-creation-vs-value-capture|creating value from capturing value]]. It also examines [[concepts/ai-commoditization-and-enterprise-lock-in|AI becoming widely available while businesses become hard to leave]].
11. **AI platforms and computing outside data centers** — [[sources/011-indrawan-nvidia-ai-pc|Indrawan #011]] presents Nvidia RTX Spark and the AI PC as [[concepts/platform-leadership-and-ecosystem-expansion|platform leadership]]. Nvidia extends CUDA from data centers toward local devices.
12. **Innovation culture and patient funding** — [[sources/012-endgame-65-indrawan-nugroho|Endgame #65]] features [[entities/indrawan-nugroho|Indrawan]]. It covers [[concepts/meaning-full-storytelling|storytelling with meaning]], [[concepts/innovation-theater-vs-real-innovation|performing innovation versus delivering real change]], and [[concepts/patient-capital-uang-sabar|patient capital (*uang sabar*)]].

## Shared bridges

These bridges connect ideas across the twelve layers:

- **Layers 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, and 9:** A strong [[concepts/fdi-climate|climate for foreign direct investment]] needs rule of law. It must also turn [[concepts/uncertainty-to-risk-translation|uncertainty into risks investors can estimate and price]]. Chatib adds limits from savings and ICOR, which measures how much investment is needed for growth. Bravo 500 adds electricity and chips. Keyu adds trade-war rerouting, domestic investment, and local governments able to absorb new opportunities through competition.
- **Layers 2, 5, and 12:** Purbaya, Chatib, and Gita describe a low ratio of money to GDP. Martyn explains that deep technology can take a decade to build. Indrawan adds the importance of funding duration. Together, they argue for more money and greater [[concepts/patient-capital-uang-sabar|patience from investors and founders]].
- **Layers 3 and 9:** The [[concepts/decentralization-paradox|decentralization paradox]] works through the [[concepts/mayor-economy|mayor economy]]. Tom cited Keyu, while Endgame #239 is the primary source.
- **Layers 3 and 4:** Both treat failure as useful. [[concepts/risk-as-innovation-fuel|Risk can fuel innovation]], while [[concepts/scientific-serendipity-and-failure|luck and failure can advance science]]. Layer 12 adds that organizations should open doors instead of relying on prizes.
- **Layers 2, 3, 5, 6, and 7:** Foreign investment can attract and spread talent. This requires [[concepts/meritocracy-vs-patronage|advancement by merit]] and [[concepts/brain-gain-and-circulation|talent gain and circulation]]. Teachers and storytellers provide [[concepts/soft-infrastructure-teachers|social foundations]]. A strong STEM environment and directed LPDP scholarships support the same goal.
- **Layers 3, 12, and 8:** Storytellers are part of society’s supporting foundations. The [[concepts/meaning-full-storytelling|storytelling with meaning]] standard connects with Jiang’s view of [[concepts/attention-as-wealth|attention as wealth]] and his concern about captured attention.
- **Layers 5 and 9:** Martyn contrasts merit with patronage in Indonesia. Keyu describes China’s talent recruitment and mayors who understand technology.
- **Layers 3 and 6:** Missing policies for the middle class, combined with inequality, can increase populism. This connects the [[concepts/middle-class-squeeze]] with [[concepts/idea-contest-and-centrism|open debate and political centrism]].
- **Layers 6, 7, and 9:** Government 4.0, flexible relations with several powers, and Keyu’s willing coalitions all support managed rivalry. See [[concepts/multipolar-balancing-and-asean]] and [[concepts/weaponized-interdependence-and-substitutes]].
- **Layers 7, 9, and 10:** [[concepts/copy-with-pride-application-layer|Adapting proven applications locally]] connects with [[concepts/process-and-diffusion-innovation|improving and spreading existing innovations]]. DeepSeek signals [[concepts/crisis-innovation-and-tech-restrictions|innovation caused by crisis and technology restrictions]]. Indrawan adds that creating value does not guarantee capturing it. Mass industrialization and US business lock-in shape who captures the gains.
- **Layers 7, 10, and 11:** As AI models become common products, value moves to harder-to-replace layers. These include business workflows in #010 and CUDA-powered edge platforms in #011. Both still face [[concepts/ai-electrification-threshold|electricity limits]].
- **Layers 12, 10, and 11:** All three come from Indrawan. Endgame #65 provides the cultural and funding foundation. His essays apply communication skills to AI strategy.
- **Layers 7 and 8 — Contested altitude:** One view focuses on building infrastructure, national control, and electricity. The other warns that [[concepts/techno-marxism|Techno-Marxism]] could capture human consciousness. Both discuss the same AI technology from different levels.
- **Layers 6, 9, and 8 — Contested frame:** One view focuses on nations balancing several powers and managing rivalry. Jiang instead focuses on [[concepts/elite-overproduction|conflict among too many elites within countries]].
- **Layers 3 and 8:** Tom sees open debate as a way to build culture. Jiang emphasizes resisting captured attention and the [[concepts/journalism-fracture|breakdown of a shared news narrative]]. Both connect social foundations with Great Books education and the message “you matter.”

## Working theses

> Finding source passages does not build understanding by itself. A maintained wiki summarizes each source once and stays current. Its knowledge grows over time. RAG, or retrieval-augmented generation, searches the source material again for every question.

> *(Attributed — Purbaya)* Indonesia’s main near-term problems are domestic, not mainly “global uncertainty.” The system has too little money, expectations are damaged, and practical barriers block investment. Two domestic growth engines could produce about 6.5%. Reaching about 8% requires foreign direct investment after the investment climate and talent improve.

> *(Attributed — Tom)* Social foundations, especially teachers, matter as much as physical capital. Incentives should reduce profits from licensing and encourage an [[concepts/ownership-society|ownership society]]. Culture must allow people to take risks and make those risks measurable. Serious media and open debate can change how people think before politics and strategy change.

> *(Attributed — Levitt)* Biological intelligence offers useful lessons. Diversity helps survival because nobody knows the future. Academic disciplines are categories created for teaching. Scientists may be wrong 90–99% of the time, yet useful failures still move science forward. AI tutors can help young people challenge established ideas.

> *(Attributed — Martyn)* Expert-led business requires advancement by merit and openness to outsiders. The main barriers are people in unsuitable roles and venture capital circulating within closed groups, not simply a “skills gap.” Indonesia should attract foreign investment and talent. It should build exportable deep technology over decades. Money alone cannot solve weak management or due diligence.

> *(Attributed — Chatib)* When monetary and budget policy have little room, growth depends on foreign investment and legal certainty. Improve the tax ratio through compliance and administration, not symbolic rate changes. Give the middle class specific support to avoid the Chile paradox. Falling behind in Government 4.0 is more dangerous than falling behind in Industry 4.0. ASEAN and relations with several powers can help Indonesia avoid choosing only the United States or China.

> *(Attributed — Bravo 500 panel)* A country needs electricity use near 10,000 kWh per person to become a major AI creator. Until Indonesia reaches that level, it should control data through cryptography and adapt proven AI applications locally. It should not wait for huge local data centers or a national DeepSeek. It should also keep the option to use both US and Chinese technology stacks.

> *(Attributed — Jiang)* True wealth is attention and consciousness, while power directs attention. AI could enable Techno-Marxism: companions and tokens might reward obedience inside a Matrix-like system. Societies produce too many elites, who then fight for influence, including finance against Silicon Valley and AI. Journalism no longer provides one shared story, so people must seek truth themselves. Speculative geopolitics remains outside this thesis.

> *(Attributed — Keyu)* China combines centralized politics with decentralized business competition through the [[concepts/mayor-economy|mayor economy]]. Western textbooks often underestimate this government capacity. Improving and spreading existing technology can matter as much as inventing something entirely new. Restrictions can also trigger innovation during crises. The US–China relationship is a rivalry to manage, not a conflict between opposite civilizations. Using economic connections as weapons encourages alternatives. Southeast Asia can benefit from global restructuring, not deglobalization, if it builds savings, education, stability, and domestic investment.

> *(Attributed — Indrawan essays)* Disrupting an industry does not mean owning its profits. DeepSeek made cheap AI a new model. As efficient AI became common, profits moved toward business integration and the costs of switching providers. Platform leaders expand early, as Nvidia moved from CUDA toward AI PCs before the category became clear. Today’s success can hide tomorrow’s risks.

> *(Attributed — Indrawan #65)* Indonesia lacks storytellers with meaning, not people who can narrate. Public displays of innovation are not the same as delivered change. Open access matters more than prizes, and technology is only the outer shell. Long projects need *uang sabar*: patient investors and patient founders. A low money-to-GDP ratio and funding cycles of about seven years make Amazon-scale timelines structurally difficult.

## Gaps / next

- Most Endgame material still comes from interviews. Independent sources should verify claims about money growth, money-to-GDP ratios, and STEM conversion rates. They should also check education spending, foreign investment, tax gaps, middle-class shares, and permit density. Energy figures need checking too, including GW, kWh, data-center MW, and LLM energy multipliers. Keyu’s figures on student exchanges, manufacturing share, and inflation passing into prices also need confirmation.
- Several Indrawan claims need independent support. These include Nvidia’s $593 billion one-day figure and the DeepSeek V4 market story. RTX Spark specifications and Computex partners also need checking. For #65, verify the bootstrapped company’s identity, the manager and staff innovation survey, and the comments about nuclear power and Bitcoin energy use.
- Useful comparison pages could examine several pairs. One could compare Purbaya, Tom, Martyn, and Chatib on routes to growth. Others could compare Bravo 500 sovereignty with Jiang’s Techno-Marxism, or multipolar balancing with conflict among elites. Further options include **Keyu’s mayor economy versus Tom’s Indonesian decentralization gap**, Indrawan on creating and capturing value versus Keyu on process and diffusion, and **#65 patient capital versus Martyn’s deep-technology long-termism**.
- The comparison between Bio-X or random seed grants and Indonesia’s STEM pipeline remains unexplored.
- Great Books education and the message “you matter” have not been compared with Indonesian teacher foundations and meaningful storytelling.
- The SBY, Jokowi, and Prabowo era stubs need independent sources.
- Levitt’s COVID claims remain contested-only. They should receive more weight only after adding dedicated epidemiology sources.
- Jiang’s speculative geopolitics remains only on the source page until verified. This includes Iranian end-times beliefs, election integrity, and the Board of Peace.
- Bhumi Varta, Sinarmas, and the panelist biographies still rely too heavily on interviews and remain stubs. Indrawan’s biography is fuller after #65, but company details are still limited.
- The schema may eventually need `policy`, `episode`, `panel`, and `essay` conventions if media ingestion becomes routine.
- Search tools such as qmd remain deferred.

## How to grow this

Put a source in `raw/` and say **ingest** to expand the wiki. Ask questions and save the most useful answers. Run **lint** regularly to check the wiki’s health.
