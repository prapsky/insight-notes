# Scientific Serendipity and Failure

Claim ([[entities/michael-levitt|Michael Levitt]], [[sources/004-endgame-193-michael-levitt|Endgame #193]]): scientific careers are chains of **unintended consequences**; Nobel outcomes are unpredictable luck; **failure is a core part of success**.

## Pattern (from his arc)

1. Snooker night → mother’s “skip school” challenge → early matriculation
2. London TV (*Thread of Life*) → molecular biology itch
3. Kendrew’s **no** → business-school **counteroffer** → Cambridge interview
4. Forced Weizmann year (he resisted) → Warshel collaboration → Nobel chain
5. Hitchhiking friend + Crick books → early network access

Tight comic punchline: “very tight connection between playing snooker as a child and getting the Nobel Prize.”

## Norms he wants for the young

- Curiosity of a 5-year-old
- Openness / take chances / do what you love
- Young people are precious disruptors — seniors “know too much” (NIH first-grant age rose ~20 years over ~30 years, per his telling)
- **Wrong 90% / great scientists wrong 99%** — harder questions; fear of mistakes kills innovation
- Use AI as personal tutor to experiment, fail, learn

## Bridge in this vault

Pairs with Tom Lembong’s [[concepts/risk-as-innovation-fuel|risk as innovation fuel]] — entrepreneurship failure without criminal stigma ↔ scientific failure as the normal path to hard questions. Different domains, same cultural requirement: make productive failure survivable.
