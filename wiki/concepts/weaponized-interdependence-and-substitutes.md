# Weaponized Interdependence and Substitutes

[[entities/keyu-jin|Keyu Jin]] argues in [[sources/009-endgame-239-keyu-jin|Endgame #239]] that closely connected economies give each side more **power over the other**. But using that power encourages the targeted side to find alternatives, and modern substitutes can appear quickly. Causing short-term pain does not guarantee long-term victory for the dominant power.

## Instances she cites

| Leverage | Induced response |
|----------|------------------|
| Dominance of the US dollar, SWIFT, and the Federal Reserve | Creates urgency to build alternative payment systems and currencies |
| China’s controls on rare-earth exports | Encourages other countries to develop alternatives and substitute materials |
| Historical cases: Napoleon’s continental system; Britain versus Germany and the US; the US versus Japan | Attempts to suppress competitors fail in the long run |

## Related claims in the same interview

- A US narrative of being the “victim” ignores its dominance in service exports and the advantages of the dollar.
- Tariffs and the splitting of supply chains raise prices in the Global South because Chinese intermediate goods are widespread. The claimed Chinese share of manufacturing is about 30%. Goods rerouted through Mexico, Canada, or the European Union often still contain Chinese inputs.
- No dominant power remains dominant forever. The spread of knowledge and AI shortens the time needed to catch up.
- Diplomacy based on “jungle rules” creates lasting damage to credibility. Allies do not simply forget when a new president arrives.

## Vault bridges

- This argument aligns with [[concepts/multipolar-balancing-and-asean|multipolar balancing]]. Third countries and middle powers try to preserve several options as bilateral pressure and the use of economic ties as weapons increase.
- It complements [[concepts/crisis-innovation-and-tech-restrictions]]. Technology bans are another case in which the use of leverage encourages the target to create a substitute.
- At a different level of analysis, the argument conflicts with Jiang’s focus on civil war among elites within countries ([[concepts/elite-overproduction]]). Keyu instead treats **rivalry between states and managed competition** as the primary frame.
- [[sources/010-indrawan-perang-ai-china-vs-amerika|Indrawan #010]]: DeepSeek remained competitive despite chip restrictions. This is an AI example of pressure creating a substitute. He adds that the US is still trying to widen its advantage in chips and cloud computing and to lock users into its ecosystem. Bans alone do not decide who captures the market.
