# Soft Infrastructure — Teachers

[[entities/tom-lembong|Tom Lembong]] argues in [[sources/003-endgame-248-tom-lembong|Endgame #248]] that no country advances without investing in **soft infrastructure**, meaning people and institutions. Indonesia’s main constraint is **teachers**: their pay, status, and skill. Building more roads and ports in imitation of China is not enough.

## Why teachers

- Pre-college years are most formative; university is more self-directed
- Few household heads have university education. In Gita’s Sabang-to-Merauke description, about 88% lack tertiary education. The future leadership pool will remain small unless school quality improves widely.
- Persistently low pay blocks cultural change. Examples include about Rp2.8 million for a teacher and about Rp500,000 for an honorary teacher.

## Budget paradox

The law assigns about **20% of the national budget** to education. In the example, that is about Rp800 trillion from a Rp4,000 trillion budget. But this does not mean the money reaches teachers. Large portions reportedly fund other programs, such as ministry-run vocational and certification courses. Programs with clear job outcomes, such as pilot training and technical certificates, could use **education loans** instead. This would free public money for teacher pay and classroom tools.

More than **70%** of education spending is spread across over 500 local governments. Their priorities differ, and the education ministry does not control all teacher-hiring standards. The center should provide common templates and measurable performance indicators without ending decentralization. See [[concepts/decentralization-paradox]].

## Against hard-infra maximalism

After the global financial crisis, many admired China’s investment-heavy model. China now faces excess capacity, empty infrastructure, and poorly allocated capital. Human and institutional capacity was never optional there either. Indonesia still gives teachers too little weight compared with toll roads, ports, and industry.

## Chatib (#216): storytellers + open hiring

[[entities/chatib-basri|Chatib]] and Gita make the same point about universities. A teacher who can **tell a clear story** attracts more students than an expert who cannot communicate, though subject knowledge is still required. University quality depends on faculty quality. Singapore uses endowments to hire world-class professors. Indonesia should hire beyond its own alumni networks and let diaspora academics teach part of the year. Serious podcasts can partly replace an education culture missing from many homes, where the same figure suggests about 88% of household heads lack tertiary education.

## Bravo 500 panel (#007): AI-era personalization + private participation

Gita reprises Sabang–Merauke ~88% and teachers as injectors of ambition / imagination / practiced serendipity. [[entities/budi-gunadi-sadikin|Budi]]: Singapore preschool coding buses = 10–20y horizon; Indonesia can’t wait on government alone — private curricula (e.g. [[entities/sinarmas|Sinarmas]] education) must aim at that horizon. [[entities/sai-prasad|Sai]]: personalized learning outside school extends creativity; institutions still supply knowledge and intent to think.

## Jiang (#008): Great Books + “You matter”

Different pedagogy, same soft-infra spirit: [[entities/jiang-xueqin|Jiang]] teaches Great Books (Plato, Homer, Dante, Milton, Shakespeare) as portal to meaning/consciousness; message to developing-economy audiences — **you matter**, choose to wake up and seek wisdom ([[concepts/attention-as-wealth]]). Complements storyteller-teachers without speaking to Indonesian pay/budget composition.

## Indrawan (#65): meaning-full vs gossip narrators

[[entities/indrawan-nugroho|Indrawan]] sharpens the storyteller ask: Indonesia already has mass narrators (TikTok, gossip TV); the gap is [[concepts/meaning-full-storytelling|meaning-full storytelling]] that activates idealism — plus teaching history as fuel for *making* future history, not only past events. Soft infra here is narrative *standard*, not only teacher pay.
