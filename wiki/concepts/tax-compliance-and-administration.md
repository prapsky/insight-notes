# Tax Compliance and Administration

[[entities/chatib-basri|Chatib Basri]] argues in [[sources/006-endgame-216-chatib-basri|Endgame #216]] that Indonesia’s low tax ratio, about 10–11% of GDP, is mainly a **tax compliance and administration** problem. It is not mainly a problem of tax rates being too low. A World Bank-style estimate puts the unpaid tax gap at about **6.4 percentage points of GDP**. Closing it could raise the ratio toward 17%.

## Why rate theater fails

- Raising tax rates brings little extra revenue if the number of people and firms that actually pay does not grow.
- Cutting rates in the hope of expanding that base also fails when **compliance** remains low.

## Admin redesign (AER evidence he cites)

Joint work with Ben Olken (MIT) and Rema Hanna (Harvard), *American Economic Review*:

- At a small KPP Pratama office, one account representative may handle about 4,000 taxpayers or 150 firms. Auditing all of them is impossible.
- Officers therefore focus on about 10 leading firms or 100 people. The burden becomes concentrated, and growing firms may **split themselves** to avoid attention.
- Moving the largest taxpayers to **KPP Madya** offices with more officers spreads the burden and can raise substantial revenue at low cost.
- The Ministry of Finance began adding KPP Madya offices, reaching about 38 from around 2022–23 in Chatib’s account. He says this network must keep expanding so tax officers do not merely keep “hunting in the zoo,” or repeatedly targeting the same visible taxpayers.

## Digital stack

- Use **Coretax** and measurable digital business processes, while expecting early problems.
- Exchange **data across agencies**, including banking, e-commerce, and vehicle records, to identify taxpayer profiles. In this framing, “data is the new oil.”
- A **KOTI**-style digital integration program could overcome resistance caused by ministerial ego. In the interview’s timeframe, the president had requested this work and a team was being formed.
- The same profiling method can target **government spending**. For example, the government could combine PLN electricity bills identified by customer name, Susenas household survey data, and e-commerce records. It could then provide electricity-bill subsidies instead of BLT, or direct cash assistance.

Better collection creates budget room for the growth and [[concepts/fdi-climate|FDI]] agenda. This matters because debt service already crowds the budget. Chatib illustrates the burden with about Rp800 trillion in interest and principal payments in #216.
