# Tax Compliance and Administration

Claim ([[entities/chatib-basri|Chatib Basri]], [[sources/006-endgame-216-chatib-basri|Endgame #216]]): Indonesia’s low tax ratio (~10–11% of GDP in his framing) is mostly a **compliance / administration** problem, not a missing rate hike. World Bank-style tax-gap story: gap ~**6.4pp of GDP** → path toward ~17% if closed.

## Why rate theater fails

- Raise rates → little extra revenue if the **base** doesn’t widen
- Cut rates hoping the base expands → fails when **compliance** is low

## Admin redesign (AER evidence he cites)

Joint work with Ben Olken (MIT) and Rema Hanna (Harvard), *American Economic Review*:

- At small offices (KPP Pratama), one account representative may “hold” ~4,000 taxpayers / ~150 firms — cannot audit all
- Officers chase the top ~10 firms / ~100 people → burden concentrates; growing firms **split** to stay under scrutiny
- Moving top taxpayers to **KPP Madya** (more officers) spreads the burden → significant revenue at low cost
- MoF began adding Madya offices (~38 from ~2022–23 in his telling) — must keep expanding so the office isn’t only “hunting in the zoo”

## Digital stack

- **Coretax** / measurable IT business process (early friction expected)
- Cross-agency **data exchange** (banking, e-commerce, vehicles) for profiling — “data is the new oil”
- **KOTI**-style digital integration (presidential ask / team forming in interview timeframe) to overcome ministerial ego
- Same profiling logic for **spending targeting** (e.g. PLN by-name billing × Susenas × e-commerce → electricity bill subsidies instead of cash BLT)

Enables fiscal space that [[concepts/fdi-climate|FDI]] and growth theses need when debt-service already crowds the budget (~Rp800T interest/amortization illustration in #216).
