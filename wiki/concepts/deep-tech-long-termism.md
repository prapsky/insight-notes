# Deep-Tech Long-Termism

[[entities/martyn-terpilowski|Martyn Terpilowski]] argues in [[sources/005-endgame-272-martyn-terpilowski|Endgame #272]] that Indonesia’s last technology cycle rewarded **short-term consumer businesses based on total market size** and fundraising through connections. Lasting growth requires **intellectual property that can be exported**, products built around engineering, and projects that take about ten years. Good management and careful investigation must matter more than simply having capital.

## Short-termism pathology (as described)

- Companies copy US consumer apps and use Indonesia’s “280 million people” as their entire market argument. This approach creates little intellectual property that can succeed abroad.
- Venture capital circulates within the same network. Investors follow famous names, accept weak company records, count paid-for revenue, and fund friends and family.
- Founders and venture capitalists sell shares as valuations rise. They move into wealth or government while the companies continue losing money.
- Investment tickets can be too large for the available opportunities. This forces investors to concentrate their money in a few “unicorns.”
- Martyn is skeptical of government bets on startups when the government has little room in its budget.

## Long-termism markers

| Marker | Contrast |
|--------|----------|
| Advanced technology or business-to-business product | Small-business apps, symbolic financial inclusion, and copied AI agents |
| Engineering at the core | A marketing-heavy company presented as “technology” |
| Regional export plan | Indonesia-only scale behind connections |
| Use one’s own capital and therefore bear personal risk | Keep raising outside money; after failure, move into adviser or venture-capital positions |
| Work on a roughly ten-year horizon | Pursue quick wealth; move from McKinsey to becoming a founder within months |

[[entities/bhumi-varta-technology|Bhumi Varta Technology]] is his working counterexample. It is expanding into Vietnam and Japan and converting distributors. Vietnamese examples include FPT, TMA, and VinFast. They show a path based on returning talent and absorbing knowledge from foreign investment.

## Money is not a complete solution

This argument agrees with, but makes more specific, several wiki themes about capital:

- [[concepts/money-supply-vs-interest-rates|Money growth]] can let more people fund ideas, as in Gita’s money-to-GDP argument. But a boom still destroys value when capital is allocated badly.
- [[concepts/fdi-climate|Foreign direct investment (FDI)]] should build real capabilities through chips, electric vehicles, and deep-technology partnerships. Martyn considers this more useful than protecting middlemen.
- [[concepts/risk-as-innovation-fuel|Risk as fuel]] requires *honest* failure. It should not allow people to regain prestige after destroying companies without accountability.
- [[concepts/patient-capital-uang-sabar|Patient capital]] (Indrawan #65): even when money is available, capital that must return within **7 years** cannot support Amazon-like projects. US pools may wait 20–30 years. The time horizon supports Martyn’s call for decade-long building.

## Pivot hope

A painful technology downturn may force a move from short-term thinking to long-term thinking. This will happen only if established elites tolerate the discomfort created when self-made outsiders succeed and foreign firms help the country absorb knowledge ([[concepts/meritocracy-vs-patronage]]).
