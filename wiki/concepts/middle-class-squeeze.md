# Middle-Class Squeeze

Claim ([[entities/chatib-basri|Chatib Basri]], [[sources/006-endgame-216-chatib-basri|Endgame #216]]): many governments (incl. Indonesia) target the **poor** with social protection and let the **rich** capture growth gains, with **no specific policy for the middle** — the Chile paradox / Sebastian Edwards framing he cites after hearing Michelle Bachelet at a Harvard ministerial forum.

## Indonesian pattern he cites (2019–2023)

| Group | Income growth (his telling) | Policy experience |
|-------|----------------------------|-------------------|
| Bottom ~40% (deciles 0–4) | Still positive, slowing | BLT, PKH, social protection |
| Middle ~deciles 5–8 | **Negative** | Largely outside PIT; tax breaks don’t bite |
| Top ~20% | ~+4% | Enjoy growth / high-end consumption (e.g. iPhone concerts) |

Middle-class population share (his series): ~5% (2003) → ~18% (2014) → peak ~21–23% then ~17% (2023). Definitions contested; insist on **granular** decile reading, not averages.

Maps to Branko Milanovic’s **elephant curve**: dip in the middle, rise at the top — explains the apparent paradox of weak middle purchasing power alongside luxury demand.

## Why tax relief fails this band

Spending band he uses for the squeezed middle (~Rp800k–9.9M/month household framing in transcript) is mostly **below the income-tax net**, so PIT holidays don’t help. Need **targeted non-tax tools** (example he backed: electricity discount for ≤2,200W households as PPN rose).

## Broader inequality stack

He pairs the squeeze with:

- Inequality of opportunity, wealth, and income
- **Centripetal** (not centrifugal) development — primary cities accelerate vs secondary/regions
- Internet/AI and bank credit limits that **elitize** capital despite “financial inclusion” rhetoric

Long-run antidote he names first: education and training — see [[concepts/stem-talent-pipeline]], [[concepts/soft-infrastructure-teachers]]. Political risk: inequality feeds rightward, fear-based populism (closing arc of #216).
