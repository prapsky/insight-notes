# Investment Debottlenecking

Investment debottlenecking means removing barriers that investors face **in practice**, even when the written rules look clear. [[entities/purbaya-yudi-sadewa|Purbaya]] sees this as a major constraint on moving growth from about 6.5% to about 8%.

## Rules on paper versus reality

Governments often add a new rule for every failure. The obstacle then appears solved on paper. In practice, complaints, demands for unofficial payments, and failures to carry out the rules continue. Domestic actors may also **reject** foreign projects even after the terms improve, as in his naphtha-cracker story.

## The task-force model he cites from 2016–19

- Hold case hearings every week and choose which complaints to handle.
- Hear hundreds of cases. About 193 were resolved, with a claimed investment impact of about Rp830 trillion.
- Yasonna Laoly chaired the task force, and Purbaya was deputy chair.
- Under Prabowo, the proposed restart would use several working groups. They would cover priority spending, private-sector barriers, and regulatory cleanup.

## How to make decisions stick

The Finance Ministry should do more than pay bills. It could delay or withhold transfers when ministries do not carry out agreed actions. That is a **credible consequence**. Regular reports to the president would add pressure. Earlier meetings lacked such consequences, so promised rule changes often never happened.

## Related issue: incentives behind permits (#248)

[[entities/tom-lembong|Tom]] says scarce permits are also a **profit incentive** problem. Parties and officials can make money from recommendations. Clearing individual cases is therefore not enough. His complementary solution combines an [[concepts/ownership-society|ownership society]] and party finance with task-force-style [[concepts/decentralization-paradox|local empowerment with accountability]].
