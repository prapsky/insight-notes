# Ownership Society

[[entities/tom-lembong|Tom Lembong]] connects this policy and cultural idea to the Anies campaign circle in [[sources/003-endgame-248-tom-lembong|Endgame #248]]. The goal is a **society in which many people own assets**. People benefit when those assets rise in value, an effect called the wealth effect. Officials should also gain more from long-term prosperity created by **good policy** than from short-term payments for permits.

## The licensing trap

Too many permits reflect three forces: **money**, **entrepreneurial culture**, and **bureaucracy**. When corruption is built into the system, officials have little reason to shorten the process. Each recommendation or exception can be sold. Unclear rules also let officials later use accusations or threats to extract money.

## Two proposed breaks

1. **Party finance** — Pay much of the operating cost of political parties from the national budget. Parties would have less need to place people in ministries mainly to make money from policy and permits. Tom says this is controversial and not a complete solution, but difficult to avoid.
2. **Long-term ownership** — Create ways for officials to own assets that rise in value when rules are clear, permits are cheap, and institutions are honest. The present value of clean long-term growth should exceed the value of extracting money every week. Leaders must first be able to imagine this stable arrangement.

## Wealth effect

From a Wall Street view of advanced economies, much prosperity comes from rising portfolios and property values, not only wages and bonuses. Asset owners therefore share an interest in a healthy and stable economy.

## Link to FDI / debottlenecking

This idea addresses the same investment-climate problem as [[concepts/investment-debottlenecking|removing investment bottlenecks]] and improving the [[concepts/fdi-climate|foreign direct investment climate]]. Its emphasis, however, is on redesigning incentives through party funding and personal ownership stakes. It does not focus only on having a *satgas*, or task force, clear individual cases.
