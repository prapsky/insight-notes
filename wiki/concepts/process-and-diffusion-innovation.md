# Process and Diffusion Innovation

[[entities/keyu-jin|Keyu Jin]] argues that **who invents a technology first** can matter less than **how widely that useful technology spreads**. She also treats major reductions in cost or production steps as a form of innovation. She cites Bezos on this point. Source: [[sources/009-endgame-239-keyu-jin|Endgame #239]].

## Why it matters

- Breakthroughs that can transform many parts of society, such as electricity and AI, are rare in human history.
- Developing countries often need technology that is **affordable and easy to adopt** more than they need a new frontier invention.
- Jin says China is strong at solving problems, reverse engineering existing products, and creating alternatives at **production scale**. Its strength is not simply making “another app.”
- The combination of **massive scale, production capability, and cutting-edge technology** is difficult to copy. She suggests India might reach it later.

## Bridges in this vault

- This supports [[concepts/copy-with-pride-application-layer|copy-with-pride]] for Indonesia. Localize proven tools and spread them widely instead of chasing a national DeepSeek for prestige.
- It differs from [[concepts/deep-tech-long-termism|deep-tech long-termism]], which focuses on exportable intellectual property. The two can work together: build intellectual property where possible, and spread cost-effective technology stacks elsewhere.
- It pairs with [[concepts/crisis-innovation-and-tech-restrictions]]. Export controls accelerated China’s crisis-driven process innovation. DeepSeek is one signal of this pattern, not the only example.
- [[sources/010-indrawan-perang-ai-china-vs-amerika|Indrawan #010]] presents the AI race as **mass industrialization** through cheap model operation and broad distribution. China uses open-source software and app ecosystems to spread AI. The United States follows a competing path through [[concepts/ai-commoditization-and-enterprise-lock-in|enterprise lock-in]]. Solar panels and electric vehicles offer an analogy: innovation came first, then China expanded mass production and distribution.
