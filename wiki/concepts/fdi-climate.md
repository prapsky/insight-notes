# FDI Climate

The FDI climate is the set of conditions that makes foreign direct investment arrive **without a country having to beg for it**. [[entities/purbaya-yudi-sadewa|Purbaya]] connects it to a path from about 6.5% to 8% growth. [[entities/tom-lembong|Tom Lembong]] compares trusted investment destinations such as Singapore and Switzerland. Both name **rule of law** and [[concepts/uncertainty-to-risk-translation|turning uncertainty into priced risk]] as basic conditions. [[entities/martyn-terpilowski|Martyn]] (#272) adds two ideas: absorb foreign knowledge like a **sponge**, and avoid fake local-partner requirements. [[entities/chatib-basri|Chatib]] (#216) sees FDI as the **remaining engine of growth** when domestic savings and room for monetary or fiscal policy are limited.

## Growth ladder (Purbaya’s arithmetic)

1. Fix domestic money and confidence. Restart private and public investment. This could produce about **6.5%** growth by combining an SBY-style private drive with a Jokowi-style state push.
2. Reaching **8%** would require tens of billions of US dollars in extra activity. That needs foreign capital on top of domestic demand, which he estimates at about 80–90% of the economy.
3. If growth and the reform direction look credible, foreign investors will compete to enter, provided the climate improves.

## What “climate” means here

- The country must [[concepts/investment-debottlenecking|remove bottlenecks]] in practice, not only on paper.
- Investors need confidence in the rule of law.
- Institutions must [[concepts/uncertainty-to-risk-translation|turn uncertainty into risks that investors can estimate and include in prices]].
- The country needs enough [[concepts/stem-talent-pipeline|engineers]] to keep new projects moving. Incentives brought Pegatron to Batam, but finding 150 electrical engineers remained the main bottleneck.
- Action against domestic blockers and illegal or unfair imports, so local firms are not crushed first.

## Trajectory over perfection

Investors do not need every reform to be finished today. They enter when a credible sequence makes **tomorrow look better than today**. Capital markets may react first, and FDI follows.

## Tom’s additions (#248)

- Singapore attracts about $100–140 billion a year, compared with about $30 billion for Indonesia. The gap per person is very large.
- Trust means safety from fraud, bank failure, seizure of property, and arbitrary use of the legal system.
- Scarce permits and income extracted from licensing damage the investment climate. See [[concepts/ownership-society]].
- A culture that permits people to take risks and fail without criminal stigma complements the ability to price risk. See [[concepts/risk-as-innovation-fuel]].

## Martyn’s additions (#272)

- **Be a sponge first:** Intel’s 2005 Vietnam investment was 100% foreign-owned. Japan used foreign finance experts and then built local capability. China hosted foreign firms, learned, and copied. Local-content rules that supply chains cannot meet discourage builders.
- Requirements for conglomerate partners, golden shares, or political partners frighten limited partners that must avoid political exposure. Local promotional stories, such as golden visas or comparing Bali with Singapore, often do not look investable abroad.
- The rule of law and the ability to hire scarce, experienced foreign workers are practical prerequisites ([[concepts/brain-gain-and-circulation]]).
- [[concepts/meritocracy-vs-patronage|Meritocracy]] is part of the investment climate because top talent will not enter a market where achievement is not recognized.

## Chatib’s additions (#216)

- When room for monetary and fiscal policy is limited, **foreign direct investment is the remaining growth engine**, represented by the “I” for investment in the growth equation. The incremental capital-output ratio (ICOR), which estimates how much investment is needed to produce additional output, and the savings figures imply that domestic savings cannot fund 7% growth alone ([[concepts/money-supply-vs-interest-rates]]).
- He cites the same Southeast Asian gap as Tom: Singapore receives $100–140 billion, while Indonesia receives $25–31 billion. About $40 billion more FDI could add about 3 percentage points of growth and still leave Indonesia below Singapore.
- During the US–China trade war, Vietnam benefited from redirected investment. Indonesia did **not** show the same pattern. Without reform, it may receive dumped Chinese goods without gaining relocated factories.
- He jokes that Indonesians “become religious” when dealing with government because permit timing cannot be predicted. This is pure [[concepts/uncertainty-to-risk-translation|uncertainty]].
- **Incentives cannot substitute for legal certainty.** Large energy and AI projects need blended finance and risk reduction by multilateral institutions before very large private funds will invest. A weak rule of law still blocks that capital.
- Indonesia should maintain a geopolitical position that preserves several options. See [[concepts/multipolar-balancing-and-asean]].

## Bravo 500 panel (#007): electrons + chips as climate

FDI in AI and data centers depends on [[concepts/ai-electrification-threshold|power]], including electricity wheeling, green supply, and seven-year power-plant lead times. It also depends on access to chips from firms such as TSMC. Policy must decide whether to accept [[concepts/physical-vs-cryptographic-sovereignty|cryptographic sovereignty]] while local physical infrastructure catches up. Private builders such as [[entities/sinarmas|Sinarmas]] already add megawatts. The national climate still decides whether Indonesia becomes a place that creates AI or remains only a consumer.

## Keyu (#009): SEA window + own investment

- Trade-war **rerouting**, rather than full deglobalization, builds supply chains in third countries. Chinese FDI into Southeast Asia and Mexico is “momentous.”
- Investors need **macroeconomic and political stability** and a credible exchange rate. China historically attracted capital despite having a different political system.
- Use digital systems to reduce red tape and improve permits, transparency, and predictability. This creates a chance to leap ahead.
- **Domestic private and national investment** are also required. FDI alone is not enough. East Asian successes also shared savings, education, and industrialization.
- Gita distinguishes China as an allocator of inexpensive **technology capital** from the West as a source of **financial capital**. Western capital is blocked where uncertainty cannot be converted into risks that investors can price ([[concepts/uncertainty-to-risk-translation]]).
- The ability of local governments to compete for firms is itself part of the climate. Places organized around this [[concepts/mayor-economy|mayor-economy]] competition can absorb capital that is relocating.
