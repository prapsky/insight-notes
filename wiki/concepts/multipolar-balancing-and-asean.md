# Multipolar Balancing and ASEAN

[[entities/chatib-basri|Chatib Basri]] and [[entities/gita-wirjawan|Gita]] argue in [[sources/006-endgame-216-chatib-basri|Endgame #216]] that Indonesia should **avoid choosing only the United States or China** for as long as possible. A choice may eventually be forced, for example by incompatible internet-of-things systems. Until then, Indonesia should join several groups and strengthen **ASEAN as one negotiating bloc**.

## Instruments named

| Instrument | Role in their framing |
|------------|----------------------|
| **BRICS** participation | Balance other groups and prevent a G7-dominated club from capturing all gains |
| **OECD** track | Engage in parallel, while accepting questions about “which side” Indonesia supports |
| **RCEP** | ASEAN-as-bloc vs China/US; more “developing economy pulse” than TPP-era framing Gita rejected as too far ahead of domestic sellability |
| **Three-way talks** among the US, China, and Indonesia | Official and semi-official talks on digital policy, the energy transition, and critical minerals. Most Indonesian nickel is Chinese-owned, which creates a problem for access to the US market. |

China is a source of technology. The G7 and United States are sources of capital, supported by their large money supply. China’s dual-circulation policy and the Belt and Road Initiative delivering less than promised in Southeast Asia limit how quickly China exports capital. Indonesia must still negotiate using its location, minerals, and market size.

## Soft power as hard constraint

In an era of economic security and moving supply chains to friendly countries, **foreign policy leads** and economics supports it. This is common in advanced economies. Indonesia needs soft power and an ability to explain its story. India’s diplomacy in many directions is one model. Singapore’s ambassadors are asked to explain Southeast Asia because Singapore both delivers results and communicates them. Teaching Indonesians to **ask sharp questions** in the AI era can strengthen that soft power. See [[concepts/stem-talent-pipeline]].

## Bilateralization risk

As multilateral cooperation weakens, one-to-one deals favor stronger countries. Gita illustrates this with a tailor who needs ten coats while another party can make only five and therefore demands the tailor’s factory. ASEAN’s collective bargaining was partly created to avoid this trap. Chatib says developing countries are naturally disadvantaged in purely bilateral deals. RCEP and ASEAN therefore still matter, even as smaller-group and bilateral tactics grow.

## Bravo 500 panel (#007): tech stack optionality

Same Gita thesis at AI infra altitude: SEA (~700M people, ~$4T GDP) must keep **optionality** — not be cornered into US *or* China. [[entities/sai-prasad|Sai]] (Google): coexistence with Chinese players is workable; pick best available tech; AI is ~**10%** of the problem (engine vs airline — brand, ops, safety, customers are local). Complements [[concepts/copy-with-pride-application-layer]] and [[concepts/physical-vs-cryptographic-sovereignty]].

## Keyu Jin (#009): rivalry, restructuring, coalition of the willing

[[entities/keyu-jin|Keyu]] aligns with the Chatib/Bravo/Gita **nation/club balancing** altitude:

- The US and China face rivalry, competition, and miscommunication, but they are not civilizational opposites. A workable arrangement is possible because direct confrontation would carry existential risks.
- Trade war = **restructuring of globalization**, not deglobalization; Chinese FDI to SEA/Mexico as third-country opportunity
- China as cheap **tech capital allocator**; West as economic capital (blocked where uncertainty unpriceable — [[concepts/uncertainty-to-risk-translation]])
- Vast majority wants multipolarity/multilateralism; middle powers (Indonesia) thrive; Asia suited to regionalization
- Weaponized finance/tech leverage spawns substitutes ([[concepts/weaponized-interdependence-and-substitutes]]); jungle-rules diplomacy loses credibility path-dependently

## Contested: Jiang (#008) — civil war within states

> **Contested (2026-08-01):** [[entities/jiang-xueqin|Jiang]] argues the primary conflict is **intra-elite civil war inside nation-states** (esp. US), not multipolar war among nations — external moves as domestic-power expressions ([[concepts/elite-overproduction]]). Chatib/Bravo/**Keyu** keep **nation/club/stack balancing** (and managed interstate rivalry) as the actionable SEA frame. Both can be partially true at different altitudes; do not overwrite.
