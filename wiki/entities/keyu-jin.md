# Keyu Jin

Keyu Jin is an economist at the London School of Economics. She wrote *The New China Playbook: Beyond Socialism and Capitalism*. She was a guest on [[entities/endgame-podcast|Endgame]] [[sources/009-endgame-239-keyu-jin|#239]] with [[entities/gita-wirjawan|Gita Wirjawan]].

## Background (as she tells it)

Jin left China at age 14 to attend Horace Mann School in New York. This was unusual at the time, and she was the only international student in her year.

She says her Chinese education taught her discipline for exams. Her US education, including lessons from history teacher Mr. DeVito, taught her to question authority and keep an open mind. She combines both perspectives.

According to Gita's introduction, her father was a Shakespeare scholar and her mother was a concert pianist. Jin studied economics at Harvard. She valued its precise tools, but also used it to study history, society, psychology, and political economy.

## Why she appears here

Jin is the main voice in this wiki for China's political economy. She explains the [[concepts/mayor-economy|mayor economy]], in which local officials compete to produce results. She also describes a state that actively supports business. The same competitive system can help explain economic growth and technology development, but also debt and too much industrial capacity.

Her name had already appeared in [[concepts/decentralization-paradox]] through [[sources/003-endgame-248-tom-lembong|Tom #248]] before her own interview was added.

Jin also connects several other ideas. She treats US–China rivalry as competition that can be managed. She discusses [[concepts/process-and-diffusion-innovation|process/diffusion innovation]], which improves and spreads existing inventions. She covers [[concepts/crisis-innovation-and-tech-restrictions|crisis innovation]] under export controls and [[concepts/weaponized-interdependence-and-substitutes|weaponized interdependence]], where countries use economic links as pressure and thereby encourage alternatives. Her views on Southeast Asian development and China as a supplier of technology connect to [[concepts/multipolar-balancing-and-asean]].
