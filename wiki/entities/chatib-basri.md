# Chatib Basri

Chatib Basri is an Indonesian economist and public intellectual. He was interviewed in [[sources/006-endgame-216-chatib-basri|Endgame #216]]. He is a former Finance Minister. The interview mentions his later visiting fellowship at Harvard. He helped design the early [[concepts/stem-talent-pipeline|LPDP]] scholarship program around 2013. He has also researched tax administration and published in the *American Economic Review* with Ben Olken and Rema Hanna.

> Claims and biography details below are from that interview unless another source is linked.

## Background

Chatib was born in Jakarta on 22 August, although the year is unclear because the automatic transcript is garbled. His parents were Minang. His family discussed biographies at the dinner table. His father worked around the secretary-general level of the Ministry of Manpower and retired as a major general. His uncle, Asrul Sani, provided a literary and less rigid influence.

He attended Kanisius schools. He wanted to study theater at IKJ, but the order of choices in the Sipenmaru admissions system led him reluctantly to the University of Indonesia's economics faculty.

He completed a master's degree and then a doctorate in Australia through the Australian National University path. He received an Asian Development Bank scholarship after sending an unsolicited letter to Peter McCawley. Once the scholarship was funded, he abruptly left an English-preparation path for the United Kingdom.

He later taught, became a public intellectual, and served as a visiting fellow at Harvard after leaving the Finance Ministry.

## Main ideas in this wiki

- [[concepts/middle-class-squeeze]] explains how the middle class can be left between aid aimed at poor households and growth captured by wealthy groups.
- [[concepts/tax-compliance-and-administration]] argues that better compliance and administration can raise the tax ratio more effectively than public arguments about tax rates.
- [[concepts/government-4-0-and-agile-regulation]] says regulation often falls behind technological disruption. Broad principles may work better than detailed rules.
- [[concepts/multipolar-balancing-and-asean]] argues that Indonesia should avoid choosing only between the United States and China. It can balance through ASEAN and participation in both BRICS and OECD-related systems.
- [[concepts/fdi-climate]] and [[concepts/uncertainty-to-risk-translation]] explain why foreign direct investment and legal certainty matter when monetary and fiscal policy have little room.
- [[concepts/stem-talent-pipeline]], [[concepts/brain-gain-and-circulation]], and [[concepts/soft-infrastructure-teachers]] cover preparation for LPDP scholars, enough strong people on each campus, and openness to faculty from outside.

## Connections to other people

- With [[entities/purbaya-yudi-sadewa|Purbaya]]: both see foreign direct investment and certainty as the next limit on growth. Chatib emphasizes the savings and ICOR gap, tax compliance, and the middle class. ICOR measures how much investment is needed to produce growth. Purbaya emphasizes money growth and a *satgas*, or task force.
- With [[entities/tom-lembong|Tom]]: both discuss difficult BKPM-era permits and the importance of teachers and other soft infrastructure. Chatib adds the “Chile paradox,” in which middle-class progress can still produce political dissatisfaction when policy does not serve that group.
- With [[entities/martyn-terpilowski|Martyn]]: both discuss using networks and strong stories to attract capital. Chatib adds Government 4.0 and blended finance, which combines public and private funding to reduce risk for large energy and AI investments.
