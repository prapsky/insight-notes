# Gita Wirjawan

Gita Wirjawan is the Indonesian host of [[entities/endgame-podcast|Endgame]]. He interviews policymakers, company founders, and public intellectuals. His Luminaries and campus series also includes international scientists.

Gita is a former Trade Minister and worked around BKPM, Indonesia's investment agency. He was a peer of [[entities/tom-lembong|Tom Lembong]] ([[sources/003-endgame-248-tom-lembong|Endgame #248]]) and [[entities/chatib-basri|Chatib Basri]] ([[sources/006-endgame-216-chatib-basri|Endgame #216]]).

He has interviewed [[entities/purbaya-yudi-sadewa|Purbaya]] ([[sources/002-endgame-245-purbaya-sadewa|Endgame #245]]), [[entities/michael-levitt|Michael Levitt]] ([[sources/004-endgame-193-michael-levitt|Endgame #193]]), and [[entities/martyn-terpilowski|Martyn Terpilowski]] ([[sources/005-endgame-272-martyn-terpilowski|Endgame #272]]). He also moderated the Bravo 500 discussion with [[entities/budi-gunadi-sadikin|Budi Gunadi Sadikin]], [[entities/franky-widjaja|Franky Widjaja]], and [[entities/sai-prasad|Sai Prasad]] ([[sources/007-bravo-500-ai-panel|Bravo 500 AI panel]]). Other guests include [[entities/jiang-xueqin|Jiang Xueqin]] ([[sources/008-endgame-259-jiang-xueqin|Endgame #259]]) and [[entities/keyu-jin|Keyu Jin]] ([[sources/009-endgame-239-keyu-jin|Endgame #239]]).

## Why he appears here

Gita is the host and a recurring conversation partner across many sources.

In discussions about Indonesia, he raises gaps in the country's ability to absorb foreign direct investment. He asks about science and engineering talent, teachers, and making important ideas available to more people. He compares China and Southeast Asia and examines ways to bring skills home or keep them moving through international networks.

He also connects the amount of money in the economy to stories told by elites. Other themes include pressure on the middle class, the “Chile paradox,” and balancing between BRICS, the OECD, and ASEAN. He emphasizes realistic financing for the energy transition. He discusses an **AI electrification threshold** of about 10,000 kilowatt-hours per person, and compares China's **mayor economy** with Southeast Asia's problems of decentralization. He presents China as a supplier of technology capital and the West as a supplier of financial capital.

In science conversations with Levitt, Gita asks about crossing academic fields, making AI widely available, evolution, diversity, and curiosity among young people.

In the discussion with Jiang in episode #259, he raises open or nonprofit AI, Acemoglu's idea that technology can favor high-wage elites, the Board of Peace, Iran, and education as a source of meaning.

In the discussion with Keyu in episode #239, he asks about state capacity, improving and spreading existing inventions versus creating something completely new, population change versus productivity, and a *modus vivendi*, or practical way for rivals to live with each other.
